5. Model Methodologies & Assumptions

  • Historical Simulation (HS): Empirical quantile of rolling historical losses with linear interpolation. Expected Shortfall estimated as empirical conditional tail mean.
  • Parametric Normal: Analytical Gaussian variance-covariance model with standard normal quantile and PDF tail expectation.
  • Parametric Student-t: Maximum Likelihood Estimation of degrees of freedom nu; analytical Student-t quantile and exact closed-form expected shortfall.
  • EWMA (RiskMetrics): Recursive exponential decay covariance matrix (lambda=0.97) with normal distribution assumption.
  • Filtered Historical Simulation (FHS): GARCH(1,1) with Student-t innovations; standardized residuals resampled and scaled by 1-day ahead conditional volatility.
  • Monte Carlo Simulation: Cholesky factorization of empirical covariance matrix with multivariate normal simulation under explicit deterministic RNG seed.

6. Limitations & Known Weaknesses

  • Daily rebalancing assumption: portfolio weights are assumed to rebalance without friction or transaction costs.
  • Single-day horizon: model forecasts 1 trading day ahead; scaling to 10-day regulatory horizons via square-root-of-time introduces well-documented approximation errors.
  • Fat-tailed innovations: standard Gaussian parametric models systematically underestimate tail loss severity during market stress.
  • Static correlation assumptions: parametric models do not capture dynamic asymmetric correlation spikes during market selloffs without explicit copula models.
